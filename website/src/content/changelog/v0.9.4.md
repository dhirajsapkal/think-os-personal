### What changed

The old site answered "what is this?" in one 758-line scroll and never answered "okay, I installed it — now what?" This release inverts that. The guide tells you exactly what to do at three cadences: one command in the morning (`/thinkos-morning`), one line when you make a decision worth keeping, and twenty minutes on Sunday (`/weekly-review`). It walks your first week in plain language — including how the stub files fill themselves in as you talk, the y / n / later offer, and the `/thinkos-continue` bulk-import fast path — then closes with a Do / Don't column and a cheat-sheet showing, for each of the ten commands you'll actually use, what you type, the plain-English phrase that works instead, and what happens.

Every behavior the guide describes traces to a shipped playbook in the repo — the command markdown files, the install flow, the continuous-capture docs. Nothing on the site describes a product that doesn't exist; that's the point of the accuracy pass, and it's why the install page now admits the wizard asks five questions, not four.

The homepage was restructured, not rewritten: § I answers what / who / trust in three cards, § II gets you started in three steps, § III shows four beats from a real workday (morning brief, an automatic work-log entry, a one-line decision capture, Sunday's staleness flag), § IV keeps the three rules, § V leads the FAQ with "What do I actually do day to day?" Everything that moved off the page landed on a dedicated, linked page — a comment in the homepage source documents exactly what went where. The design system (Newsreader / Hanken Grotesk / IBM Plex Mono, editorial numbered sections, reduced-motion-safe reveals) is extended, not re-themed.

### Upgrade

Nothing to upgrade — this release only changes the website. `/thinkos-update --pull` will simply bump your local copy to v0.9.4; no instructions, skills, hooks, or vault files change.
